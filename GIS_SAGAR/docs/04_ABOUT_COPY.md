# About GIS–SAGAR

Local evidence for groundwater recharge.

GIS–SAGAR — Smart Application for Groundwater Artificial Recharge — helps people explore where recharge may be appropriate and which structures deserve site assessment.

## Why it matters

Groundwater supports homes, farms and communities. Where extraction exceeds replenishment, recharge planning needs to respond to local ground conditions. A structure that works at one location may not suit another.

## The gap we address

The project began with two connected needs: wider access to practical geospatial tools and stronger participation by groundwater users. GIS–SAGAR brings mapped evidence into a workflow that communities and technical practitioners can discuss together.

## How it works

Select a site, load observations and inspect a weighted screening score for each analysis cell. The application evaluates recharge structures against the available evidence and identifies the site checks needed before selection.

## Enabled for now

Soil texture and slope are enabled for the current assessment. The configurable framework also retains geology, geomorphology, land cover, drainage, weathering thickness, groundwater depth and rainfall for staged integration as data and assessment rules become available.

## From evidence to action

Recommendations are preliminary. Site observations and technical assessment are needed to confirm receiving-ground permeability, groundwater conditions, water quality and structural suitability. The application supports this assessment; it does not determine construction dimensions or recharge volumes.

## A community and climate purpose

By making the evidence and outstanding checks visible, GIS–SAGAR aims to support informed local participation in water conservation and climate adaptation. Its value will be assessed through site verification and future field use.

Original project proposal: Anagha Raghunandan. This application develops the proposal’s focus on technology access and community participation.
